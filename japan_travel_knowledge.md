# Japan Travel Assistant - Knowledge Base

## 1. Transportation

### Shinkansen

The Shinkansen is Japan's high-speed railway system. It connects major cities and is one of the most convenient ways to travel between large urban areas.

The Tokaido Shinkansen connects Tokyo, Nagoya, Kyoto, and Osaka. The route is useful for travelers visiting several major cities during the same trip.

### Local Trains and Subway

Large Japanese cities have extensive railway and subway networks. Tokyo has multiple operators, including JR East and several subway operators.

Tokyo Metro operates several subway lines throughout Tokyo. Travelers should check the specific railway operator and route when planning a journey because different companies operate different services.

### IC Cards

IC cards are rechargeable contactless transportation cards. Suica and PASMO are examples commonly associated with transportation in the Tokyo area.

IC cards can be used on many trains, subway systems, and buses. They can also be accepted at some convenience stores and vending machines.

Travelers should check whether a specific service accepts their IC card before relying on it.

### Airports

Tokyo is primarily served by Haneda Airport and Narita International Airport.

Haneda Airport is located closer to central Tokyo and is often convenient for travelers staying in the city.

Narita International Airport is located farther from central Tokyo and provides several railway and bus connections to the metropolitan area.

## 2. Major Destinations

### Tokyo

Tokyo is Japan's capital and one of the country's largest metropolitan areas.

Popular areas include Shibuya, Shinjuku, Asakusa, Akihabara, Ueno, and Tokyo Station.

Shibuya is known for its busy streets, shopping, restaurants, and the famous Shibuya Crossing.

Asakusa is associated with traditional Tokyo and attractions such as Senso-ji.

Akihabara is well known for electronics, anime, manga, games, and related pop culture.

### Kyoto

Kyoto is known for temples, shrines, traditional architecture, gardens, and historical districts.

Important attractions include Fushimi Inari Taisha, Kiyomizu-dera, and Arashiyama.

Kyoto can be explored using a combination of trains, buses, walking, and other local transportation.

### Osaka

Osaka is a major city in the Kansai region and is known for its food, shopping, entertainment, and urban atmosphere.

Popular areas include Dotonbori, Namba, and Osaka Station.

Osaka can be combined with Kyoto and other Kansai destinations because the cities are connected by extensive railway services.

### Hiroshima

Hiroshima is an important destination for travelers interested in Japanese history.

The Hiroshima Peace Memorial area includes the Atomic Bomb Dome and Peace Memorial Museum.

Miyajima, officially known as Itsukushima, is another major destination in the area and is famous for Itsukushima Shrine and its torii gate.

### Nara

Nara is known for its historic temples, shrines, parks, and deer.

Nara Park is one of the most recognizable attractions in the city.

## 3. Accommodation

### Hotels

Hotels are available in most major Japanese cities. When choosing accommodation, travelers should consider proximity to a railway or subway station because public transportation is an important part of traveling around Japanese cities.

### Ryokan

A ryokan is a traditional Japanese-style inn. Some ryokan provide tatami rooms, futons, and traditional meals.

The experience and facilities can vary significantly between individual ryokan.

### Capsule Hotels

Capsule hotels provide compact sleeping spaces designed for short stays. They can be useful for travelers looking for a distinctive and often space-efficient accommodation experience.

Travelers should check the property's rules regarding luggage, check-in times, and shared facilities.

## 4. Food and Dining Culture

### Restaurants

Japan has a wide variety of restaurants, from inexpensive casual establishments to high-end restaurants.

Popular foods include ramen, sushi, tempura, okonomiyaki, curry rice, udon, and soba.

### Tipping

Tipping is generally not expected in Japan. Travelers should not assume that leaving a tip is required after a normal restaurant or service interaction.

### Restaurant Etiquette

Travelers should follow the rules of the individual restaurant. Some establishments may have specific instructions regarding ordering, seating, payment, or where to place personal belongings.

### Convenience Stores

Convenience stores, commonly called konbini, are widespread in Japanese cities.

They commonly sell prepared food, drinks, snacks, household items, and other everyday products.

## 5. Japanese Cultural Etiquette

### Public Transportation

Travelers should generally avoid loud conversations and disruptive behavior on public transportation.

Phone calls may be discouraged or considered inappropriate on trains and other crowded public transportation.

### Shoes

Some places in Japan require visitors to remove their shoes before entering. This can occur in traditional accommodations, certain restaurants, temples, and private spaces.

Visitors should look for signs or follow the instructions of staff.

### Temples and Shrines

Temples and shrines are religious and cultural places. Visitors should behave respectfully and follow posted instructions.

Photography rules can vary between locations, so travelers should check local signs.

### Onsen

Onsen are Japanese hot spring bathing facilities.

Many traditional onsen require guests to wash themselves before entering the communal bath.

Rules regarding tattoos can vary by establishment, so travelers should check the individual facility's policies before visiting.

## 6. Travel Preparation

### Passport

International travelers should carry a valid passport and verify the entry requirements that apply to their nationality before traveling.

Entry requirements can change, so travelers should consult official Japanese government or immigration sources for current requirements.

### Money

Japan uses the Japanese yen (JPY).

Travelers should plan how they will obtain Japanese yen and should consider carrying more than one payment method.

### Internet Connectivity

Travelers can consider options such as international roaming, travel eSIMs, physical SIM cards, or portable Wi-Fi.

The best option depends on the traveler's phone, carrier, length of stay, and data requirements.

### Luggage

Travelers using trains should consider the amount of luggage they carry because large stations can be busy and transfers may involve stairs, escalators, or walking.

When using long-distance trains, travelers should check the current rules of the specific railway service regarding oversized baggage.

## 7. Useful Japanese Phrases

### Greetings

Hello: こんにちは (Konnichiwa)

Good morning: おはようございます (Ohayou gozaimasu)

Thank you: ありがとうございます (Arigatou gozaimasu)

Excuse me / Sorry: すみません (Sumimasen)

### Travel Phrases

Where is the station?: 駅はどこですか？ (Eki wa doko desu ka?)

How much is it?: いくらですか？ (Ikura desu ka?)

Please help me: 助けてください (Tasukete kudasai)

I don't understand: わかりません (Wakarimasen)

## 8. Emergencies

### Emergency Numbers

Japan uses 110 for the police and 119 for fire and ambulance services.

Travelers facing an emergency should contact the appropriate emergency service and, when possible, ask hotel staff or another trusted person for assistance.

### Lost Property

Japan has a well-established lost-property system. Travelers who lose an item should contact the relevant railway operator, station staff, transportation company, hotel, or local police depending on where the item was lost.

---

## 9. Trip Planning Principles

A good Japan itinerary should consider geographic proximity, transportation time, opening hours, and the amount of time available.

Travelers should avoid planning too many distant destinations on the same day.

For multi-city trips, transportation between cities should be considered before deciding how many destinations to include.

Travelers should distinguish between information stored in this knowledge base and current information that may change, such as schedules, prices, temporary closures, entry requirements, and transportation disruptions.

For current or time-sensitive information, travelers should verify details using official sources.
